## 12. Choosing real components

- **§12 — Use C0G/NP0 for filters, RF matching, oscillators/crystal loads, PLL loop filters, timing, and low-distortion coupling; typical tolerance 5% or 1% when justified. Up to approximately 220 nF/25 V in 1210.** **Artifact:** capacitor selection/BOM. `:1870-1874`
- **§12 — For X7R/X5R/Y5V, design using capacitance at actual DC bias and temperature, not nameplate value. An X5R may lose approximately 84% at rated voltage; temperature loss can reach approximately 70% from 25–75 °C. Use larger case or higher voltage rating where needed.** **Artifact:** capacitor derating calculation. `:1875-1881`
- **§12 — Do not use Class 2/3 ceramics in RC timers, frequency-setting filters, or audio coupling where value drift distorts operation.** **Artifact:** schematic/component choice. `:1881-1882`
- **§12 — Avoid piezoelectric MLCC noise beside sensitive sensors or in 1–20 kHz LED PWM/coil/burst-regulator applications; use C0G/film or switch above 20 kHz.** **Artifact:** placement and capacitor selection. `:1882-1885`
- **§12 — Select capacitor values/packages whose self-resonant frequency is above the served band; include pad/via inductance.** **Artifact:** RF/power capacitor selection. `:1886-1887`
- **§12 — Aluminium electrolytics are polarized, approximately ±20%, leaky, and aging; check ripple current at its actual frequency. Life approximately doubles for every 10 °C cooler than the 105 °C rating.** **Artifact:** power-supply BOM/thermal calculation. `:1888-1890`
- **§12 — Polymer capacitors provide low ESR, better bias stability, and long life; verify datasheet limits.** **Artifact:** capacitor selection. `:1891-1892`
- **§12 — Derate MnO₂ tantalums to half their voltage rating because they fail short and can burn.** **Artifact:** capacitor BOM/safety review. `:1893`
- **§12 — Use film for stable, accurate, low-loss/high-voltage functions; polypropylene has lowest loss; use silver mica only for unusually demanding specifications.** **Artifact:** capacitor technology selection. `:1894-1895`
- **§12 — Check resistor worst-case dissipation and voltage rating: typically 50–75 V for 0603 and 150 V for 0805. Use thin/metal film for precision low-noise/low-distortion work, low tempco, adequate power, and pulse-rated parts for surges.** **Artifact:** resistor BOM/calculation. `:1896-1900`
- **§12 — Provide copper for hot resistors, while considering the added parasitic capacitance.** **Artifact:** PCB footprint/layout. `:1900`
- **§12 — For DC-DC inductors, follow the regulator datasheet, select saturation current above peak—not average—current, and use shielded types near sensitive circuitry.** **Artifact:** power-inductor BOM/layout. `:1901-1903`
- **§12 — For RF inductors, simulate with the manufacturer’s Q/model, keep SRF well above operating frequency, and do not use an inductor above SRF where it behaves capacitively.** **Artifact:** RF matching/filter design. `:1903-1905`
- **§12 — Linear-regulator dissipation is `(Vin_max − Vout) × Imax`; calculate junction temperature at highest enclosure ambient.** **Artifact:** regulator thermal calculation. `:1906-1908`
- **§12 — If needed, use a series resistor ahead of an LDO to share dissipation/filter input, but verify lowest-input headroom remains above dropout. Example: 2.2 Ω 1206 dissipating 0.1 W.** **Artifact:** power schematic/thermal analysis. `:1908-1910`
- **§12 — For semiconductors, calculate dissipation and junction temperature at maximum ambient. MOSFET RDS(on) at 100 °C is approximately 1.5× the 25 °C value; include switching loss.** **Artifact:** semiconductor thermal/power analysis. `:1911-1913`
- **§12 — Determine θJA from the actual copper area/thermal layout, not blindly from the datasheet test board.** **Artifact:** PCB thermal design. `:1913-1914`
