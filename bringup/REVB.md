# What rev B should change

Kept during the rev A bring-up (from 2026-10-28).  Each row says what showed it: a measurement in
[`BRINGUP_LOG.md`](BRINGUP_LOG.md) (with its log entry), a reading of the board or a datasheet, or a design review whose
item the bench is still to confirm.  The architecture rev B was already given in the plan (a SYZYGY or on-board-FPGA
host with LVDS comparators: [`../docs/HOSTS.md`](../docs/HOSTS.md), [`../docs/SYSTEM.md`](../docs/SYSTEM.md), REVA_CHANGES
24) is not repeated here: this list is what rev A itself teaches.

Status: **seen** (measured or read on the built board), **to confirm** (a review's item the bench has not shown yet),
**dropped** (the bench showed it does not matter).

| 9 | The Costas loop locks 90 degrees round with the default sign jumpers (JP101 Q-, JP102 Q+): Q at its maximum, I at its comparator's threshold, sign(I) chattering.  **Cause found, a design error: Response 10 inverted the I arm** (ILO to U105's + input, IHI to its -, so the pair routes uncrossed) and its note says the loop then 'locks 180 degrees round'.  That holds for inverting the LO (I and Q together, a rotation), not for inverting I alone: that is a mirror, sign(-I) x Q = -sign(I) x Q, and the loop's feedback turns positive at 0 / 180 degrees.  With a sign() detector a wrong sign does not run away, as BRINGUP 3.3 and the schematic note expected: it holds at the detector's jumps (+-90 degrees), sign(I) chattering, which looks like a lock.  The measurements agree: last night's sine fits (`overnight/iq_20261029_163536.json`, the loop held with VC near 1.3 V, so the carrier above the RX LO) put Q MON 88-93 degrees **ahead** of I MON at every frequency from 3 to 6 GHz, which is the ADL5380's own Q lagging I behind the inverted I arm; with the PI chain's two inversions and the VCXO's positive slope that predicts exactly the lock seen.  The hardware does what its circuit says; the default jumpers are the wrong way for the circuit as drawn | board A, stage 3.3 (2026-10-29); the I/Q phase fits; gen/sch_rx.py (Response 10's note on the I arm) | on rev A: **move both jumpers** (JP101 left: Q+ to B1; JP102 right: Q- to B2), no soldering; or JP103 to FPGA with LOOP_SEL held (a plain PLL on Q).  Rev B: either un-invert the I arm or keep it and flip the default and its silkscreen; and derive the loop's sign end to end on paper (demodulator convention, both arms' polarities, the comparator, the switch's select, the PI's inversions, the VCXO's slope) in the design notes, re-derived after any polarity change | seen; cause found |

## Quality of life: set from software what rev A sets by hand (Jason, 2026-10-29)

Jason: "I spend more time away from the bench than at it, and having remote control of these things is generally better than
requiring me to come in."  Rev A's loop-sign fault (row 9) needed two jumpers moved by hand, and so did each re-cabling of
the night's tests.  Static settings need no header pins: an 8-bit I2C GPIO expander on the existing I2C bus (TCA9534 /
PCA9534 class, 0x20-0x27; the gateware's generic I2C registers already reach it) gives eight outputs.  Its outputs come up
as inputs, so pull resistors give today's jumper defaults at power-up and the board still works with no firmware.  The
expander's input register reads the state back, for the log.
